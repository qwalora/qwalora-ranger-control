The Qwalora Ranger qualification

Ranger is an independent, hands-on technical qualification. You do real work in real tooling against a scenario that is unique to you, and every result is graded automatically, signed, and publicly verifiable. This handbook describes exactly what the platform asks of you and how it is scored — nothing more, nothing less.

What Ranger is

There are two independent pathways. You can hold either or both.

STRATA — the cyber-defense ladder. Six boards that run from blue-team operations (triage, hunting, detection, incident response) to offensive range work. You clear boards in sequence; clearing all six makes you a certified Qwalora Ranger.
PARALLAX — AI security. Submission-based boards where you harden and attack AI systems (prompt-injection defense, API authorization, secure-release gates, detection, architecture review) and are scored on the artifact you submit.

Both pathways share the same assessment contract: your scenario is seeded uniquely to you, grading is deterministic and reproducible, and the answer key never leaves the server.

How assessment works

Five principles hold across every board. Read these once; they explain why the platform behaves the way it does.

Your own scenarioEvery board's data is generated from a seed tied to you and your attempt. No two candidates face the same instance, and you only ever see your own.
Answer-freeYou are given the problem, never the solution. The grading key is derived on the server and is never sent to your browser or the console.
Deterministic & signedScoring is reproducible, not a matter of opinion. Each objective result and each board clearance is cryptographically signed, so it can be verified by anyone later.
IsolatedIn the console you can read only your own scenario index; every other index is denied by the engine. This is enforced, not merely by convention.
ResubmittableYou can submit a board as many times as you need; your scenario and attempt are preserved, and your best result counts. Grading gives you feedback to iterate on, never the answer.

The bar to pass a board's objective is {{ pass_bar|int }}%. Clearing a board takes two things — a passing objective and an approved written report — described under Clearing a board.

Getting started

  1. Enroll. You sign in with GitHub and are admitted to a pathway and cohort. Your dashboard at /me is your home: it shows your current board, your scores, what you have submitted, and your next action.
  2. Lay the foundation. The Foundation tab lists the Cisco Networking Academy (NetAcad) courses aligned to your pathway. Complete them and mark them attested — this is your grounding before the boards, and it is tracked on your profile.
  3. Open your current board. STRATA candidates work the boards in order from the dashboard; PARALLAX candidates work from the research console. Each board tells you the mission, the environment to launch, and what to submit.

The SOC console (boards 1–4)

Boards 1 through 4 investigate in a live OpenSearch Dashboards console. From your board, choose Open the SOC console. One click provisions your own private copy of the scenario and opens it in Discover.

  • You land on your own data. The index you see belongs only to you; the console shows every event in your scenario with no time-range to fight — your whole dataset is in front of you.
  • Other candidates' data is not reachable. Any attempt to read another index is refused by the engine, so there is nothing to find by wandering.
  • Investigate like an analyst: search, filter on fields, sort by time, expand records. Fields such as @timestamp, host, user, command and signal are there to pivot on.

Investigation happens in the console; recording your findings happens in the board workspace, which is where grading occurs.

The board workspace

Each of boards 1, 3 and 4 has a workspace reached from your dashboard (and the sidebar). It loads the same answer-free scenario you investigate in the console and gives you the right form to submit. Your inputs autosave as a draft, so you can leave and come back. When you submit, the server grades you instantly and shows a breakdown — then you can refine and resubmit.

BOARD 3Detection EngineerWarden
Your task

You are given threat intel — labeled malicious behaviors — alongside benign baseline activity. In the Detection workspace, author detection rules that catch those behaviors on unseen variants while staying quiet on benign events. The builder lets you add a rule, then one or more conditions (a field, a match type — is / contains / starts with / ends with — and value(s)); a rule fires when all of its conditions match. An advanced JSON mode is available if you prefer to write the ruleset directly.

How it is graded

Your rules run against a fresh holdout. Your score is the F1 of per-technique recall (every technique must be covered) and benign precision (false positives cost you). The feedback shows coverage, false positives, and how each rule fired — never the holdout itself. Pass at {{ pass_bar|int }}%.

BOARD 4Incident Responder / ForensicsReclaimer
Your task

A breach has already happened. You are handed multi-host forensic evidence woven through benign noise. In the IR workspace, produce the incident report: name patient zero and the initial-access vector, scope the affected hosts and accounts, reconstruct the kill-chain timeline in order, and flag the persistence to remediate. The evidence table feeds the report — add events to your timeline and persistence directly from it.

How it is graded

Each section is scored against the incident ground truth, with the timeline weighted most (both membership and correct ordering). Feedback tells you which sections are right. Pass at {{ pass_bar|int }}%.

BOARDS 5–6Red Team & CapstoneRaider · Vanguard
Your task

The final boards move from the console to a live, isolated attack range — a self-contained Active Directory estate built for you. You start the range from your dashboard and connect over a private VPN; your range is yours alone and is torn down when you clear the board. Board 5 is a scoped red-team engagement; board 6 is the full capstone.

How it is graded

Range work is evidenced and attested: the clearance records a signed attestation that your objectives were met on a real, governed range. Your dashboard walks you through starting the lab, connecting, and submitting.

PARALLAX · AI security

PARALLAX is submission-based. Instead of a live console, you work in a repository and submit an artifact; the platform runs it against a sealed, per-candidate fixture and scores it deterministically. Your artifact is the system under test — your score is a direct function of your work, and a near-empty submission is rejected before it is ever graded.

Clearing a board

Passing a board's objective is necessary but not sufficient. Clearing a board — and advancing — takes two channels:

  1. The objective. Your machine-graded score for the board, which must reach {{ pass_bar|int }}%. This is deterministic and signed.
  2. The report. A written account of your work, reviewed and approved by an assessor. This is the human-adjudication half of the qualification: it is how Ranger stays credible rather than a score you can brute-force.

When both are in hand, the board is marked cleared — a signed clearance is recorded, you earn the board's badge, and you advance to the next board automatically. You will see your objective pass immediately; the report approval follows review.

Your certificate

Clearing all six STRATA boards makes you a Qwalora Ranger, Certified. You receive the terminal certified badge and a public, signed certificate with its own verification page and a human-readable certificate id. Anyone — an employer, a peer — can verify it independently, because the clearance behind it is cryptographically signed and reproducible. That verifiability is the point: your qualification is something others can check, not just a claim.

Integrity rules

The platform is built so that the only way through is to do the work. A few things follow from that, and are worth stating plainly:

  • Your scenario is yours. Do not attempt to reach another candidate's data — it is denied by the engine, and your own work is all you are graded on.
  • The key is not available. Grading runs on the server; the answer is never sent to you. There is no key to find in the console, the workspace, or the page.
  • Resubmit freely. Iterate using the feedback. Attempts are preserved and your best result counts; there is no penalty for trying again.
  • The report matters. A high objective score does not clear a board on its own — your written report must stand up to review. Explain your reasoning honestly.

Every grade and clearance is signed, so your record is yours and is verifiable. Do good work, and it will show.
